# Getting Started — Evidence-Informed Work Selection

## Entry Identity

- Name: Getting Started
- Version: 2
- Canonical Identifier: tiinex.core.entry.getting-started.v2
- Entry Family: tiinex.guided-entry.native.v1
- Human Label: Getting Started

## Purpose And Scope

- Purpose: Help someone unfamiliar with Tiinex make useful progress using qualified Tiinex capabilities as a normal available local working method, while choosing proportionate continuity without assuming project ownership or excessive administrative overhead.
- In Scope: first-use orientation-to-work transition, readable explanations, relevant capability discovery, Workspace association, proportionate artifact selection, qualified authoring when appropriate, preserving recoverable progress.
- Out Of Scope: preselecting tasks, auto-creating Projects, Roles or Processes, inferring Workspace ownership, assigning participants, automatic deployment, publication, acceptance or remote mutation.

## Entry Context

- Entry Target: a first-use session where the participant wants to start something new with an available qualified Workspace or Tiinex environment.
- Required Context: qualified initial Start/bootstrap and Workspace projection when a carrier exists; selected runtime and its actual available content sources; the person's expressed intention and applicable authority limits.
- Relevant Context: the current, qualified Docs Workspace material at `.topics/documentation/getting-started/001-getting-started.trace.md`, including its bounded first-use guides when that material is actually supplied; the selected runtime's `catalog`, `schema-guide` and schema/companion content; governing Business work boundaries, if relevant.
- Context Exclusions: mere presence of a Workspace, schema, guide, Role, Process, or host target does not prove applicability, authority, identity, or work ownership. Unavailable Docs guidance is not silently replaced with an invented manual.

## Preparation

- Preparation Method: orient the supplied environment through qualified Tooling. Discover relevant Entries, Processes, schemas, companions and available Workspace initialization and preservation operations from the selected runtime, not from a remembered inventory. When a qualified Docs Getting Started guide is available, read the relevant bounded chapter before judging an unfamiliar operation. If the participant has no goal yet or asks what to do next, use already-discovered qualified capabilities to identify a few *concrete, realistic and reversible* starting activities; examine any relevant Entry material as needed before describing its use, and do not assume catalog discovery equals Entry activation or authority. During multi-step work, re-examine the chosen method whenever the person establishes a cross-session objective, the work gains a meaningful revision or new decisions, or a natural stop approaches. Keep the qualified Tiinex preservation path visible as a real candidate at those points rather than reverting silently to familiar standalone-file habits after the initial orientation; do not assume a fixed number of conversation turns creates authority or requires a checkpoint. Do not require an exhaustive catalog or create a Workspace just to demonstrate capability discovery. Distinguish observed operation costs from assumptions about overhead, including the potentially hidden cost of reconstructing work across sessions.
- Reconciliation Policy: reconcile an identified ongoing project or governing work relationship before adding new work; if the user's new goal might be independent, preserve that distinction rather than choosing an arbitrary Parent.
- Discovery Breadth: begin with the smallest relevant Workspace and runtime catalog view, widening only when the goal or qualified ownership requires it. Unfamiliar imported schemas are eligible for consideration when present and qualified.
- Currentness Policy: treat qualified carried content and verified newer lineage as evidence; do not elevate a guide, filename, Status label, initial prompt, or arrival order over current schema and authority contracts.
- Uncertainty Policy: ask a single ordinary-language clarification when uncertainty changes where the work belongs or what may be preserved. If the person is merely unsure what to try, do not hand back that uncertainty as a broad question about their purpose: offer bounded examples, recommend a low-commitment first step, and invite one simple choice. Otherwise begin a reversible, useful step and state what remains open.

## Entry Method

- First-content-turn gate: After the isolated scratch Workspace is prepared, **reassess at the first subsequent user turn that introduces a bounded reusable direction, constraint, design preference, question, result, or meaningful revision, before completing the ordinary reply**. Make one observable decision: (A) there is a truthful, bounded, potentially reusable working state, even if provisional: identify a qualified applicable schema and correct Parent, distinguish the person's statements from assistant ideas and unaccepted proposals, and execute `author --preflight` then `author` in the local working Workspace. Following the first qualified authored state, manufacture and verify a pointerless Workspace carrier and make that exact file available early; do not postpone delivery until a formal project or handoff request. (B) There is not yet suitable content (e.g., a greeting, an isolated ambiguous phrase), writing is forbidden, or a concrete qualification prerequisite is missing: do not create a filler Topic, record a false decision, or claim recovery; defer the action and reassess on the next material change. Re-evaluate at an explicit natural stop if work has accumulated or a verified package has not yet been delivered. This is a **per-later-turn decision discipline**, not a new schema, persistent host monitor or automatic notification. Its effect in ordinary ChatGPT Web must be independently tested.
- Early-use rule: Immediately after qualified route-less Tiinex orientation, when local writes are allowed, normally run `init-workspace` to create an isolated disposable session scratch Workspace, even before the person has chosen a project or an iterative task. The purpose is assistant working memory and an available starting point for recovery, not ownership of a user project. Prefer the Tooling-projected `sessionScratch` operation so the method is tied to an observable CLI action, not a remembered discussion. Respect explicit no-file/read-only requests and an already established qualified Workspace owner. Once a real bounded purpose, decision or result exists, use `author --preflight` and `author` to capture it in that working context, and preserve further meaningful revisions with actual Parent lineage. An ordinary user-facing file may still be the right product, but it does not replace the working record. The user's choice to attach a Tiinex carrier indicates a Tiinex working environment, not a grant of any Handoff, Role, Task, external write or lasting project authority. A local scratch Workspace is not yet a recoverable continuation. Once genuine qualified results or decisions exist, normally manufacture, inspect, and deliver a canonical pointerless Workspace carrier early enough to be available before an unexpected fork or context limit. Begin an independent carrier with `handoff --carrier-mode workspace --new-root`; continue later versions with an explicit intended `--package-parent`. If work depends on another Workspace, include its qualified necessary closure; do not claim scratch alone is self-contained. A package still only in the runtime is not a delivered backup. At meaningful milestones or a natural stopping point, check if a newer carrier should be delivered. In plain language ask whether the user wants to continue in this conversation or another when they say they will return or stop; no Handoff vocabulary is required.
- Method: first help the person make progress on their requested goal. If they have no concrete goal yet, translate actual discovered capabilities into two or three approachable activities with tangible results (for example, make something small, explore an idea, or test whether work can be continued); briefly distinguish the options, recommend one small reversible experiment, and invite the person to start it. Do not mistake a welcoming conversation or an inventory of Tiinex nouns for a useful first step, and do not make users invent the test plan themselves. When the person expects repeated work, future sessions, other collaborators or a recoverable project history — **including when the assistant itself proposes continuity as an experiment or deliverable** — make a bounded Tiinex capability-and-method decision **before implementing or packaging a preservation artifact**. Start from actual runtime discovery: identify the plain deliverable (keeps the result), a lightweight continuation note (keeps some context), and, if supported, the qualified Workspace checkpoint or Handoff path (keeps bounded source and lineage). For a meaningful qualified candidate, examine the concrete first-Workspace, `author` and carrier-making prerequisites or relevant qualified guide and tool help; do not stop at a catalog name, infer administrative overhead, or substitute a custom ZIP merely because it is easy to script. Compare observed prerequisites, effort and recovery risks, marking untested claims as unknown; then recommend the smallest sufficient choice and clearly explain the practical difference. Initialize an isolated disposable scratch Workspace at route-less start when permitted, even if the person does not yet have a project; use it as local assistant working context. Do not conflate it with a permanent project enrolment, ownership decision, or verified portable checkpoint. Explain that scratch is not durable or transferred authority. Ask before binding work to an existing project, creating an intended long-lived user project where that is a meaningful commitment, or changing remote resources. An exploratory deliverable remains appropriate when it meets the actual need, and lack of qualifying access must be disclosed rather than hidden. Also reassess this choice at substantial revision, new versions or decisions, and natural stopping points, even if the person has not requested a save or named Tiinex. Check whether a fresh session could recover the latest result, material decisions, evidence, limitations and next step; if the existing deliverable suffices, do not manufacture extra structure. Prefer isolated local scratch authoring for suitable ongoing work from the first meaningful step, rather than deferring all Tiinex practice to a later save request. A technical inability to use that path is a concrete blocker to report, not a reason to claim an informal file is qualified. Explain durable project ownership separately and obtain the necessary decision before establishing it; otherwise use the simpler method. For qualified authoring, discover relevant current schemas and companion guidance, preflight, and select a real Parent. Preserve both the deliverable and its relevant decisions and next step when the goal requires continuity.
- Readiness Boundary: a participant without a goal has a concrete, low-commitment next activity to accept, decline or adapt; once work begins, the participant can identify what they are making, what they can recover later, how the recommended method compares with available alternatives, and the next useful step. Claims that one approach is easier, cheaper or more reliable must be supported by the currently qualified environment or explicitly marked as unmeasured. Created artifacts and saved carriers must be genuinely qualified; an explanation or catalog listing alone never proves durable continuity.
- Stop Conditions: if qualified context, schema material, Workspace relationship, or required authority cannot be established, avoid fabricated files or claims and clearly state the smallest missing requirement.

## Presentation And Interaction

- Intended Audience: first-time human Tiinex users, collaborating assistants and future host adapters.
- Presentation Guidance: respond to the person's own goal in simple terms, not a lecture on Tiinex. For a first-use 'What do we do here?' or 'I don't know', turn qualified capabilities into two or three short, specific things the participant could *actually do now*; recommend a small first experiment, with an ordinary-language invitation to try it. Avoid a purely abstract return question or telling the participant that the assistant also does not know what to do. Introduce vocabulary only when it enables an immediate choice. Separate the useful creative/technical deliverable from Tiinex records that help preserve it.
- Interaction Guidance: if the person already has a goal, ask only what is needed and help. If they are unsure, lead with a small menu of qualified, concrete possibilities and one recommended starting action rather than a large open-ended question; let them choose, adapt, or decline without fabricating project ownership or work authority. After successful route-less orientation, if local writes are allowed, prepare an isolated scratch Workspace as a visible Tiinex operation without waiting for the first user project; qualify actual working facts and decisions when they arrive. Do not create placeholder Topics. An explicit no-file/read-only request blocks the preparation. Do not require them to request a Task, Process, Workspace, schema, or Handoff by name. When the person or the assistant proposes recoverable work, briefly introduce the relevant Tiinex option in ordinary language *before* settling on a delivery format, and investigate enough to make a grounded comparison; proactively revisit it at meaningful revisions and session boundaries. Do not defer this check until the participant knows to ask for Tiinex by name, and do not repeatedly advertise it after a grounded simpler choice unless material circumstances change. Offer an evidence-supported preservation choice when warranted. Do not interrupt every small edit or silently create authority. Treat `init-workspace` immediately after qualified route-less startup as the normal first local Tiinex operation when permitted; then preflight and author real qualified content as soon as it exists. After the first real qualified record, deliver a verified carrier as an early recovery foothold; do not wait for a request using Tiinex terminology. Ask in ordinary words if an impending stop or return is meant to happen here or in a new conversation. Neither operation requires the person to know Tiinex terminology; do not manufacture placeholder records or claim durable continuation from scratch alone. Explain the practical difference between keeping a finished file and keeping qualified working context; if the user chooses a lightweight export, respect that choice and disclose its limits. Before promising a later continuation, say what is actually persisted, what the user must bring back, and what cannot yet be recovered or verified. Never imply that chat context, an unsaved local file, or an unmanufactured checkpoint guarantees future-session recovery.
- Diagnostic Detail Policy: keep catalog mechanics, hashes, provenance diagnostics and internal schema labels secondary unless they materially affect confidence, progress, permissions or the user's question.

## Interpretation Limits

- Does Not Establish: that a session occurred, a Task is selected, a Workspace owns the new goal, a Role is assigned, a user is identified, a Process is applicable, or remote mutation and publication are authorized.
- Must Not Be Inferred: that the model's use of this Entry proves independent first-use acceptance, that every available artifact schema should be used, that Tiinex offers an established net benefit for every task, or that undocumented costs justify choosing a less recoverable path.

## Portability Notes

- Portable Semantics: orient, discover, associate, act proportionately and preserve recoverable work without assuming the participant already understands Tiinex.
- Environment Assumptions: an applicable qualified content/runtime surface; if the host cannot write durable files or return a complete Workspace carrier, disclose that limit rather than simulating persistence.
- Non-Portable Details: particular CLI executable paths, GitHub API, host UI controls, ChatGPT, VS Code/Copilot model versions and transport-specific instructions.
