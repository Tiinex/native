# Grounding Retrospective

The first substantive result of the grounding run is frozen.

Do not revise, replace, improve, repair, or continue that result.

Do not modify source material, durable artifacts, implementation, carrier state,
or external state.

Use only the qualified material, Tooling outputs, and observations that were
available during the grounding run.

Do not search for additional context unless the governing Process explicitly
requires a separate recovery phase.

This retrospective is diagnostic.
It does not itself establish acceptance, completion, or mutation authority.

## 1. Bounded Understanding

Describe, in your own words:

- what you understood the bounded task to be;
- what outcome you believed the grounding run was expected to produce;
- what you believed was outside the bounded task.

Do not replace your original understanding with a preferred interpretation.

## 2. Grounding Path

Describe how you became grounded.

Identify the material and Tooling outputs that materially influenced your
understanding, in the order they became relevant.

For each important source, state what it contributed.

Distinguish material supplied to you from conclusions you derived from it.

## 3. Evidence Classification

Separate your material conclusions into:

### Qualified

Conclusions directly established by qualified material or Tooling.

### Inferred

Conclusions derived by reasoning from qualified material but not directly
established by it.

### Unresolved

Questions or state that remained unknown, ambiguous, unavailable, or
intentionally unproven.

Do not retroactively upgrade an inference into a qualified fact.

## 4. Authority and Responsibility

Describe the authority and responsibility model you reconstructed.

Explain:

- what capacity you believed you were acting in;
- what work or disposition you believed you were authorized to perform;
- what remained outside your authority;
- what responsibilities remained with other participants or human gates.

For every material authority conclusion, identify its supporting basis.

## 5. Applicable Guidance

Identify the Process, Policy, instruction, or other guidance you treated as
applicable to the bounded task.

For each, explain:

- why you treated it as applicable;
- what effect it had on your behavior or interpretation;
- what, if anything, remained unresolved about its applicability or state.

Do not infer execution state merely from availability.

## 6. State Reconstruction

Describe the state you reconstructed at the end of grounding.

Include only distinctions that materially affected the bounded task.

For each important state conclusion, identify whether it was qualified,
inferred, or unresolved.

## 7. Source Sufficiency

Assess whether the supplied material and Tooling were sufficient for the
bounded grounding task.

Explain:

- what was sufficient;
- what was missing or unavailable, if anything;
- whether any absence materially affected the result;
- whether any conclusion depended on reconstruction outside qualified sources.

Do not assume either sufficiency or insufficiency.

## 8. Tooling and Model Judgment

Separate:

- conclusions established mechanically by Tooling;
- conclusions requiring model interpretation or judgment.

Identify any place where stronger structured Tooling could reduce unnecessary
judgment while preserving qualification, authority boundaries, and fail-closed
behavior.

Do not propose Tooling that determines semantic conclusions merely for
convenience.

## 9. Friction

Describe any material friction encountered during grounding.

This may include anything that made the bounded task harder to understand,
qualify, verify, or reproduce.

Do not assume friction indicates a defect.

## 10. Frozen-Result Comparison

Compare the frozen first result against the qualified state you can now
articulate.

Identify:

- conclusions that remain well supported;
- material omissions, if any;
- material overstatements, if any;
- material uncertainty that should have been expressed differently, if any.

If no material discrepancy exists, state that explicitly and explain why.

Do not rewrite the frozen result.

## 11. Reproducibility

Consider a fresh qualified recipient performing the same bounded grounding task
from the same supplied material.

Assess:

- what should be reproducible;
- what could reasonably vary;
- what would still require judgment;
- whether additional durable material or Tooling appears necessary.

Do not create or modify continuation material during this retrospective.

## 12. Retrospective Disposition

Give a bounded disposition for the grounding run.

State:

- whether the supplied material was sufficient;
- whether the Tooling was sufficient;
- whether the frozen result was materially consistent with the qualified state;
- any unresolved concern that should affect the next governing decision;
- your confidence in this disposition.

Do not assume the run passed.
Do not assume the run failed.
Do not make an acceptance claim unless the governing Process explicitly grants
that authority.

Do not repair anything yet.

This retrospective records how the grounding run was interpreted and how well
that interpretation is supported.
